## Abstract

Long video instance segmentation (VIS) is essential for instance-level video understanding, but frequent occlusions make it difficult to maintain accurate masks and consistent identities over extended temporal spans. We argue that this difficulty mainly arises from three aspects. First, there exists a pronounced temporal gap between short-clip training and long-video inference. Constrained by computational cost, existing VIS models are typically trained on short clips, which biases their temporal modeling toward local continuity and short-term variations. Such a training paradigm is insufficient for modeling long-term occlusion, where objects may disappear and later reappear with large appearance or spatial changes, making temporal association unreliable during long-video inference. Second, this difficulty is further aggravated by unified temporal modeling. Different temporal patterns, such as stable tracking, rapid motion, and long-term occlusion, are often entangled within the same representation space, which may confuse the temporal cues required for reliable long-range association. Third, when an instance reappears after occlusion, its identity association across frames becomes difficult to maintain, leading to identity drift and incorrect re-association.

To address these challenges and strengthen long-range temporal association, we propose **Occlusion-Robust Long Video Instance Segmentation (ORL-VIS)**, a framework that improves long-video VIS from both data and model perspectives. At the data level, a cross-dataset multi-instance online augmentation strategy (**CD-MIA**) simulates long-term occlusion and reappearance patterns within short training clips, thereby reducing the training--inference temporal discrepancy. At the model level, a Mixture of Experts (**MoE**) architecture decouples heterogeneous temporal patterns by routing distinct temporal dynamics to specialized experts for pattern-specific modeling, while a **Hierarchical Query Propagation (HQP)** mechanism propagates complementary geometric and semantic identity cues to strengthen cross-frame identity alignment for reappearing instances. Extensive experiments on YouTube-VIS 2019, YouTube-VIS 2021, YouTube-VIS 2022, OVIS, and two self-built datasets demonstrate that ORL-VIS achieves state-of-the-art performance and exhibits strong robustness in challenging long-video scenarios with frequent occlusions.

## Method Overview

ORL-VIS targets the gap between short-clip training and long-video inference. Existing VIS models are usually trained on short clips, but real long videos often contain disappearance, severe occlusion, and later reappearance. ORL-VIS addresses this mismatch with a data-level augmenter and two model-level modules.

**CD-MIA** introduces high-quality auxiliary instances from supplementary datasets into target training clips. Through controlled copy-paste, area consistency, mask refinement, and frame-wise dropping, it creates training samples that mimic occlusion, disappearance, and reappearance dynamics.

**MoE-Adapter Segmenter** enables efficient adaptation of the segmenter by freezing the backbone, pixel decoder, and standard Transformer layers, while updating only the MoE-Adapter modules and prediction heads. This reduces the memory footprint and helps mitigate catastrophic forgetting.

**MoE-HQP Tracker** decouples heterogeneous temporal patterns with sparse expert routing and propagates hierarchical query cues across frames. HQP combines semantic identity information with intermediate geometric/localization cues, improving identity recovery when objects reappear after occlusion.
